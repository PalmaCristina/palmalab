Palma Lab · Research

Research

Microorganisms constantly encounter changing and stressful environments. To survive, they must sense these changes, process information, and dynamically reorganize cellular functions.

The Palma Lab investigates the regulatory mechanisms that enable microorganisms to sense, respond to, and adapt to environmental stress. We combine mathematical and computational modeling with quantitative experiments to understand how gene regulatory networks generate robust and adaptive cellular behaviors.

Microbial Stress Sensing, Response & Adaptation

We investigate how microorganisms detect environmental perturbations and translate them into coordinated cellular responses. We are particularly interested in understanding how stress-response networks balance immediate survival with longer-term adaptation.

Dynamics of Gene Regulatory Networks

Cellular responses are inherently dynamic. Gene expression can change continuously, switch between states, or generate transient responses such as pulses.

We study how the architecture and kinetics of gene regulatory networks generate these behaviors and how dynamic regulation influences microbial phenotypes.

Predictive & Quantitative Systems Biology

We develop mechanistic mathematical models of gene regulatory networks and integrate them with quantitative experimental data.

Models allow us to test competing hypotheses, identify regulatory mechanisms that are difficult to observe directly, and generate experimentally testable predictions.

Synthetic Biology as a Tool to Understand Regulation

We use synthetic biology approaches to perturb and redesign regulatory systems. By systematically modifying network architecture and measuring the resulting cellular behavior, we can uncover the principles governing natural biological regulation.

Our Approach

Our research integrates experimental and computational approaches across population and single-cell scales.

  • Mathematical modeling of gene regulatory networks
  • Deterministic and stochastic simulations
  • Quantitative gene expression measurements
  • Single-cell analysis and flow cytometry
  • Time-resolved plate-reader experiments
  • Microscopy and image-based measurements
  • Synthetic biology and genetic perturbations
  • Model calibration, parameter estimation, and data integration
